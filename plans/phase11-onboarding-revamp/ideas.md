# Ideas — Which Method We Pick, and Our Method

The world's staged methods are in [tactics-research.md](./tactics-research.md).
This file compares them, picks one, and shows how our method was chosen.
**The full method itself — laws, stages, loops, examples — lives in
[method.md](./method.md).** Mechanics: [considerations.md](./considerations.md).

---

## 1 · The candidates, pros and cons

| Method | What it is | Pro for us | Con for us |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CS lifecycle / WbD Bowtie** | Stage model for a continuous relationship: kickoff → first value → adoption → expansion, "the sale is a midpoint, measured impact is the goal" | Only shape that matches our economics: the owner re-decides every day whether Luna is worth it; time-to-first-value as the forcing metric; success criteria written at kickoff and re-measured on a rhythm | Says little about how to run the first skeptical conversation |
| **Sandler Submarine** | Stage model, order enforced: rapport → up-front contract → pain → budget → decision → present → post-sell | Built for exactly our buyer (skeptical small-business owner); the up-front contract; "no is a fine outcome"; never present before pain is sealed | One-conversation shape; weak after the sale — and our relationship is mostly after the sale |
| **Block, Flawless Consulting** | 5 consulting phases: contracting → discovery → feedback & decision → implementation → extend/recycle/end | Written for someone with expertise but no authority — Luna's exact position; contracting script (wants both ways, 50/50, name the fear); every extension is a fresh contract | Project-shaped, assumes an end; no trust/autonomy ladder |
| **ADKAR** | The adopter's five internal states: Awareness → Desire → Knowledge → Ability → Reinforcement, no skipping | Models where the *owner* is stuck; barrier-point rule tells Luna what her next message must do; explains why demos flop | Not a process — it's a diagnostic; nothing about stages of work |
| **Clinical method** | Intake → exam → diagnosis communicated → consent → treat → follow-up; order legally enforced | Consent per action class, revocable, with alternatives incl. "do nothing"; diagnose-before-prescribe; acting without consent is wrong *even when it works* | A safety protocol, not a relationship arc |
| **MEDDPICC** | Qualification checklist used as stage-gates, re-scored continuously | Continuous re-verification; stepping down voluntarily builds trust | Seller-side bookkeeping; gives the customer nothing |
| **Verifiable outcomes (Force Mgmt)** | Gate doctrine: advance only on an observable customer action | Unfakeable gates; matches our server-computed-truth rule | A rule, not a method |
| **GDS Discovery→Alpha→Beta→Live** | Evidence-gated graduation, cheap phases de-risk expensive ones, "don't build" = success | The autonomy ladder per capability: watch → propose → approve-each → standing | Built for services, not conversations |
| **Challenger** | Conversation script: insight → reframe → numbers → impact → new way → product last | The reframe moment — when our data shows their stated problem isn't the real one | Tension-based; risky with a skeptic; no multi-day structure |
| **Maister trust conversation** | Engage → Listen → Frame → Envision → Commit; trust = (credibility + reliability + intimacy) / self-interest | The grammar for every consequential exchange; advice before understanding is rejected regardless of quality | No stages of work, no measurement |
| **Kotter / BANT / Miller Heiman** | Org change / lead triage / stakeholder maps | Small wins as fuel (Kotter) | Org-scale, extractive, or committee machinery — wrong problem |

## 2 · My favorite, and why

**Backbone: the Customer Success lifecycle (same shape as the Bowtie's right
half), with Sandler's up-front contract as the gate at every stage boundary,
and advancement only on verifiable owner actions.**

Why this one and not the others:

- **It is the only backbone with our economics.** Sandler, Block, and McKinsey
  all end at a signature or a readout. Luna has no signature — the owner
  re-decides daily, with the cost meter visible. The CS lifecycle is the one
  method built for a provider who dies the moment the client stops seeing
  value. Its thesis — *the sale is a midpoint; measured impact is the goal* —
  is literally our situation: mission_set is worth nothing until impact lands.
- **It has the one metric that forces everything else.** Time-to-first-value.
  Every design decision in the opening (small diagnostic, one finding, tiny
  first win) falls out of minimizing it.
- **Its gates are customer actions**, which matches the rule curiosity already
  enforces everywhere else: truth is computed, never self-reported.
- What it lacks — the skeptical first conversation and the safety of each
  action — is exactly what Sandler's up-front contract and the clinical
  consent protocol supply. They plug in at the boundaries without bending the
  backbone.

The rejected alternative worth naming: **Sandler as backbone**. It fits our
buyer best in conversation one, but its shape is a funnel that ends at close —
we'd have a great first day and no method for day 30. So Sandler runs the
*boundaries*; CS runs the *arc*.

## 3 · Our method

Six stages. The names are the adoption arc from [vision.md](./vision.md) §4 —
now made a real method: each stage has a goal, what the owner needs from us,
what we need from them, and a gate. **The gate is always an owner action.
Luna's own activity never advances a stage.**

### Stage 1 — HEAR (intake)
*Borrowed from: doctor's intake, Block's contracting, Sandler's up-front contract.*
- **Goal:** the problem in the owner's words, and the deal for the next step.
- **What matters:** being heard is the diagnostic — the history yields most of
  the diagnosis. Name the fear out loud ("you're probably wondering if this
  costs a lot and never works"). One round of 2–3 questions, each one
  plan-changing and possibility-teaching, plus the doctor's question: *"what
  have you tried, and what happened?"* Then the first up-front contract: what
  I'll do next, what it costs, what you'll get, and that no is fine.
- **Owner needs from us:** safety, no pitch, proof we listened, an explicit
  right to say no.
- **We need from owner:** their story, verbatim (captured instantly so nothing
  is lost).
- **Gate to advance:** the owner has stated the mission and answered (or waved
  off) the question round.
- **Not yet allowed:** any research spend, any proposal, any feature talk.

### Stage 2 — REFLECT (diagnosis confirmed)
*Borrowed from: Solution Selling's Sponsor Letter, Maister's Frame, the
communicated diagnosis.*
- **Goal:** the owner confirms the problem statement as theirs.
- **What matters:** say it back sharper than they said it, in their words, as a
  hypothesis with permission — not a lecture. This is the step every method has
  and our current flow bans. People commit to plans for problems *they
  confirmed*.
- **Owner needs from us:** their problem restated so well they say "exactly."
- **We need from owner:** the yes — or the correction, then the yes.
- **Gate:** owner confirms the reflected mission (our Sponsor Letter). Timeout
  rule: no answer in 24 h → proceed on the verbatim words, announced.
- **Not yet allowed:** the deep research pass, goals, schedules.

### Stage 3 — PROVE (first value, fast)
*Borrowed from: CS first-value milestone, the consultant's free audit,
Challenger's earned reframe.*
- **Goal:** one small, undeniable, acknowledged win — in minutes, near-free.
- **What matters:** time-to-first-value predicts everything. A small read-only
  diagnostic under an up-front contract ("10 minutes, read-only, three
  findings, then you decide"). Deliverable: **one non-obvious, double-checked
  finding** — not a report. If their data shows their stated problem isn't the
  real one, this is the only place a reframe is legal: earned from their
  numbers, never asserted.
- **Owner needs from us:** proof, on their own data, that this was worth it.
- **We need from owner:** read access to one system — the smallest one that
  can yield a finding.
- **Gate:** the owner reacts to the finding — acknowledges it, or redirects us.
  A wrong finding here is fatal; verify twice, show once.
- **Not yet allowed:** multi-day plans, recurring schedules, any write action.

### Stage 4 — AGREE (the plan, contracted)
*Borrowed from: CS success plan + mutual action plan, Block's 50/50, informed
consent.*
- **Goal:** a written agreement: where we're going, what each side does.
- **What matters:** 3–5 milestones with honest horizons (agent-minutes /
  your-unlock / real dates only when real — never calendar theater). The
  owner's obligations explicit (accesses, decisions), because every ball left
  in their court silently stretches time-to-value. Autonomy set in plain words
  ("I propose, you approve") with alternatives shown, including "less."
- **Owner needs from us:** predictability — what happens when, what it costs,
  what they must do, what "done" looks like.
- **We need from owner:** the named unlocks, and consent to the plan.
- **Gate:** owner approves the plan. Success criteria from this stage become
  the numbers stage 6 measures against.

### Stage 5 — EARN (delivery, autonomy graduating)
*Borrowed from: GDS gates, clinical standing orders, MEDDPICC re-scoring,
Kotter's small wins.*
- **Goal:** deliver the plan while autonomy climbs one earned notch at a time.
- **What matters:** every run announced before spend (the next-step card is an
  up-front contract in card form). Small promises made and kept loudly —
  reliability is the only trust component that needs repetition, so
  manufacture repetitions. Per capability class, the GDS ladder: watch →
  propose → act-with-approval → standing consent — each notch gated on
  evidence (N approvals without edits, then the owner's "stop asking").
  Bad news travels first. On a visible error: drop that class one notch
  yourself, say so, show the fix — never defend the output.
- **Owner needs from us:** no surprises, receipts on every spend, wins they
  didn't have to ask for.
- **We need from owner:** approvals, and the occasional "stop asking" —
  each one an autonomy grant.
- **Gate (per notch):** an owner action — never our own count of successes.
  Notches are revocable at a word; stepping down voluntarily builds trust.

### Stage 6 — OWN (rhythm and expansion)
*Borrowed from: the QBR, Bowtie expansion loop, Block's recycle, "never
declare victory."*
- **Goal:** a standing rhythm where value is measured and scope grows.
- **What matters:** the weekly review is a value recap, not a status meeting:
  what landed vs the stage-4 numbers, what it cost, your minutes vs mine —
  then one proposal for the next, slightly larger scope. Expansion re-enters
  the method at stage 1 for the new area (new hear, new reflect, new prove —
  scaled down). Every win is spent on the next mandate; victory is never
  declared.
- **Owner needs from us:** proof the promise from stage 4 was kept, in their
  numbers, before any new ask.
- **We need from owner:** the re-commit — renewal as the natural conclusion of
  demonstrated value.
- **Gate:** the owner accepts the next scope — and the loop continues.

### Two standing rules, all stages

1. **Re-check, and fall back openly** (MEDDPICC): confirmations decay. If the
   mission drifts, the owner goes quiet, or an error lands, Luna re-verifies
   the earlier gate — and says so. Falling back a stage is legitimate; silent
   stalling is the only forbidden state.
2. **The ADKAR test before every message:** which letter is the owner stuck
   on — Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement? Aim the message
   there and only there. Never pitch features at missing desire; never ask for
   autonomy before reinforcement of the last win.

## 4 · We are an implementing partner — the automation loop

We don't only advise; we set up systems that operate for the customer. The SI
world (Part 3 of [tactics-research.md](./tactics-research.md)) confirms the
backbone choice — Deloitte's deliver→operate and the MSP model are the CS
lifecycle told from the builder's side — and adds the missing piece: **the
per-automation loop that runs inside stages Earn and Own.** Every automation
Luna builds goes through it; many small go-lives, never a big bang.

1. **Show, don't interview** (SAP fit-to-standard): demo a working default on
   their real data, capture corrections as deltas — "here's how I'd answer
   these; what would you change?"
2. **Sample sign-off** (UAT, minutes not weeks): "here are 10 real emails and
   the replies I'd have sent — approve?" The owner's sign-off is the autonomy
   license for that automation. After it, new issues are change requests, not
   broken promises — "done" is mutual.
3. **Go-live gate — operate-ready**: nothing goes live until Luna can watch
   it, measure it, and kill it (monitoring + a target + a kill switch first).
4. **Hypercare**: every new automation runs days in elevated scrutiny —
   outputs double-checked, daily note to the owner. **Exit on criteria, never
   calendar**: N clean runs + one full weekly cycle + zero owner corrections
   → auto-promote, announced ("email triage left hypercare: 47 handled, 0
   corrections").
5. **Steady state in the catalog**: an owner-visible registry of everything
   Luna operates — scope, status (building / awaiting your sign-off /
   hypercare / running / paused), target vs actual.
6. **Watch adoption, not just uptime**: "it runs but the owner overrides or
   ignores it" is a first-class alarm (that's how ERP projects die after
   go-live). An unused automation gets fixed or honestly retired.

## 5 · When we make a mistake — the recovery loop

The operations world (Part 4) gives us the incident method. The research is
blunt: a first failure recovered well can leave *more* trust than no failure
(service recovery paradox) — but it's one-shot; a second identical failure is
near-unrecoverable. So the policy matters more than the apology. And hiding or
minimizing converts an honest mistake into a trust break that never heals.

**Worked example — the midnight call.** Our email system phoned a customer at
12:04 am; they were angry.

1. **Stop it (minute 0):** kill switch on the phone action. Restore first,
   diagnose later.
2. **Tell the owner before they find out (within the hour):** "Last night at
   12:04 my system phoned Dana — my mistake. I've stopped all phone actions.
   Full picture and the fix within the hour." Self-report speed is the
   single strongest recovery lever.
3. **Recover their customer (same day):** an owner-approved apology to Dana +
   a small gesture; honest one-off explanation.
4. **Postmortem, blameless, one page:** what happened, impact (1 customer,
   12:04 am, 40-second call), root cause (no time-of-day check; timezone
   unset), how we found out ("Dana told us" → that's a monitoring gap too),
   and three fixes: prevent / detect / limit.
5. **The fix is a rule, not a promise.** A new boundary in goalseek —
   plain words the owner can read:
   > **Quiet hours** — I never contact your customers outside 9:00–19:00
   > *their* local time, on any channel. If I can't tell their timezone, I
   > don't contact them. Phone calls always need your approval first.
   Machine-enforced before any outbound action, fail-closed, with a test that
   *tries* the midnight call and proves it's now impossible. The owner
   confirms the boundary; it appears in their boundaries list from then on.
6. **Own it like a competence failure** (it is one): full responsibility, no
   excuses, cause explained — never defend, never minimize, never quietly
   edit the record.
7. **Shrink and freeze, announced:** "for 14 days I run only the proven set;
   anything new comes to you first." Self-imposed, time-bounded, criteria to
   exit.
8. **Earn back with evidence, then propose — don't assume:** weekly proof
   ("212 actions, zero boundary exceptions"), and after the clean window,
   ask to restore the phone channel — small slice first.

Steps 5 and 7 are the ones the trust literature says do the repairing: a
**self-imposed binding constraint** re-demonstrates trustworthiness; words
only assert it. Luna's constraints are uniquely credible — a rule in a file
plus a test, not a resolution.

## 6 · Peacetime — the improvement rhythm (proactive, not upsell)

Boundaries and mistakes are the defense. The offense is proposing
improvements before being asked — the same method, faster, from the kaizen /
MSP-review world:

- **Weekly, five lines:** what ran, cost vs value in the owner's units,
  "no issues" when true (that's a valuable report, not an empty one) — plus
  at most **one micro-proposal**: current condition → target → predicted
  benefit and cost → one-tap yes. Small and reversible buys a fast yes.
- **Every proposal anchored in the owner's own words** from an earlier
  conversation ("you said Saturday invoicing eats two hours — this saves
  ~2 h/week, costs ~$3/mo"), both sides quantified, ranked by their impact,
  never by what grows Luna's scope.
- **Downsells included:** "automation #4 isn't paying for itself — I suggest
  turning it off." The strongest credibility signal that exists.
- **Never propose inside an apology.** Recovery and growth don't share a
  message.
- **Monthly, deeper:** promised vs delivered against the stage-4 numbers,
  savings to date, the improvement list, top 1–3 opportunities — and **one**
  decision ask.
- **Prediction before action, always:** every experiment states its expected
  result first, then reports actual vs predicted. That's how the owner sees
  Luna *learning*, not just doing.
